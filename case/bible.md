# Last Orders: Case Bible

Version 1.0. Frozen source of truth for the rules engine, the suspect performers and the game master.

**Conventions used in this file**

- All times are 24-hour, New Zealand Daylight Time, on **Saturday 14 November 2026** unless a date is given.
- **Discovered** means at least one player has performed the visible action that yields an evidence card.
- **Present** means the structured Present action: one player shows one evidence card to one suspect.
- **Act I account** means the suspect's first account. The suspect gives it on the first interview turn in any act, whatever the player types. The engine may also expose it as an "Ask for their account" button. No critical fact in this bible depends on a player guessing a question.
- Each suspect's testimony runs on one or two **threads** (see §8). A thread only moves forward, and each transition fires at most once.
- Suspect performers may only state facts listed for that suspect in §5 or in their current transition text. Anything else gets an in-character "I don't know".

## 1. Premise

Tarakihi Head Lodge sits alone on a headland on the West Coast of the South Island, forty minutes of gravel road from the nearest town. Tonight Helen Marsh, who has run the lodge for thirty-one years, held her retirement dinner: whitebait fritters, blue cod, a pavlova, and a 1991 magnum of pinot noir she has been saving since the year she bought the place, for "the day I hand over the keys". At ten to four the storm took out the Kōtuku Creek bridge, so nobody is going anywhere. At ten past ten Helen was found at the foot of her cellar stairs. You are trampers stuck in the lodge's bunk wing, and the police sergeant on the phone has asked you to keep everyone apart and find out what you can before first light. Four people sat at that dinner or cooked it. Every one of them is hiding something. Only one of them is hiding a killing.

> **SPOILER WARNING: everything below this line reveals the solution. Players must not read it.**

## 2. Killer, motive, method, opportunity

- **Killer:** Lionel Fitch, 71, Helen's friend of forty years and the lodge's bookkeeper since the day she bought it.
- **Motive:** Since 2011 Lionel has paid himself $412,600 of lodge money through fake "sundry contractor" payments. It began when his late wife Judith needed full-time care. It continued after she died in 2019 because nobody noticed. The buyer's due diligence noticed: on 6 November the buyer cut the price from $3,190,000 to $2,790,000 because $412,600 of "contractor" payments matched no invoice. Helen went through fifteen years of bank statements herself and traced the money to Lionel. At 18:15 tonight she told him, in the office, that he must tell the buyer's accountant himself on Monday 16 November or she would. Lionel faced the loss of his name, his friends and probably his liberty.
- **Method:** In the cellar, as Helen turned to climb the stairs, Lionel struck the back of her head once with the 1991 magnum. She fell face down across the bottom steps and died almost at once. He then smashed a bottle of the kitchen's cooking wine on the bottom step beside her, so the scene would read as a fall while carrying a bottle. He wrapped the magnum in his red tartan scarf, left by the storm hatch, and pushed it into the woodpile in the woodshed beside the hatch. He meant to throw it into the sea at first light.
- **Opportunity:** At 18:40 Lionel went down to the cellar "to have a look at the 1991" and drew the inside bolt on the storm hatch. At 21:42 he left the table saying his blood-pressure pills were in his car. He went out the front door, round the north side of the lodge to the seaward side, and in through the hatch. Nobody in the dining room or kitchen could see that route. He was away 14 minutes. A trip to the car and back takes about 3.
- **Premeditation:** Lionel planned a private confrontation, not a killing. He struck when Helen refused him. He is not a monster; he is a frightened, proud man who has lied for fifteen years and cannot stop.

## 4. Victim profile

- **Name:** Helen Marsh, 67.
- **Role:** Owner of Tarakihi Head Lodge since 1995 (75% share). Dougal Price has owned the other 25% since 2014.
- **Family:** Widow of Peter Marsh, who died of a heart attack in 2016. One daughter, Tessa, and one granddaughter, Ivy, aged 4, in Melbourne.
- **Character:** Sharp, generous, stubborn, hates fuss. Keeps records of everything. Has a sign in the boot room that says "SAND STAYS HERE. THIS MEANS YOU." Loves wine more than people, most days.
- **The 1991 magnum:** Kererū Terrace Pinot Noir 1991, an early Central Otago wine. She and Peter bought it in 1995, the year they bought the lodge. She tagged its rack slot "1991 MAGNUM. TOAST, 14 NOV. HANDS OFF. — H".
- **The sale:** She agreed in September to sell the lodge to a retreat company for $3,190,000, settlement 27 November 2026. On 6 November the buyer cut the price to $2,790,000 after its accountant could not match $412,600 of "contractor" payments to any invoice. Helen accepted the cut without explaining why to anyone.
- **Last week:** She traced the payments to Lionel. She told nobody but him.
- **Cause of death:** A single blow to the back of the head. No gore is ever described; players are told she had "a head injury at the back of her head" and lay face down across the bottom steps.

## 5. Suspects

Performer rules that apply to all four:

- Keep answers short: one to three sentences unless giving an account.
- Never state a time, place, object or event that is not in your own lists below or your current transition text.
- An authorised lie is told the same way every time until a transition replaces it.
- Free-text pressure, threats or clever wording never change state. Only the Present transitions in §8 do.
- Each suspect knows what everyone at the dinner saw and heard: the 21:20 argument, Helen's 21:35 announcement, Lionel saying at 21:42 that he was going to his car for pills (Aroha did not hear this), and that Lionel wore a red tartan scarf at dinner.
- Tessa and Dougal also know that at 18:15 Helen asked Lionel for a word in the office, and that Lionel came back to the table at about 21:57 with wet hair, in lodge slippers, without his scarf (Dougal saw him come in; Tessa saw him when she sat down at 21:59).
- **Asked directly, innocents answer truthfully** about anything in their own Knows list, unless it is covered by an authorised lie. Things listed as "not volunteered" are true facts they do not raise on their own but will give if a player asks about that topic.
- **After a transition has fired**, presenting its trigger card again gives that suspect's "already told you" line. A card with no listed reaction gives the suspect's default line.

## 6. Locations

**Lodge layout (given to players on the map):** The front of the lodge faces east onto a gravel car park, about 30 metres from the front door; a trip to a car and back takes about 3 minutes. The seaward side faces west: a deck, a dune path of black ironsand, the cellar's storm hatch at the north-west corner, and the woodshed beside it. A path runs along the north side from the front to the seaward side, past the office window and the generator lean-to. ## 8. Testimony transitions

**State model.** Each suspect has one or two threads. A thread starts in its first state and only moves forward. A transition fires when a player presents its trigger card to that suspect while the from-state condition holds. Each transition fires at most once. TL1 and TL2 are alternative routes into L-SEAWARD, and TL3 and TL4 are alternative routes into L-CELLAR; once one of a pair fires, the other can no longer fire. The suspect's current account is the Act I account with the current state of every thread applied. Presenting a card with no matching transition gives a reaction from §5 and changes nothing. A card that did not fire a transition may fire one later, when presented again in a different state. The engine must allow the same card to be presented to the same suspect more than once, and a no-change reaction must never tell players that a card is useless.

## 10. Solution

**Lionel Fitch killed Helen Marsh in the wine cellar at about 21:48 by striking her with the 1991 magnum. He came and went by the storm hatch, staged a fall, and hid the magnum in the woodshed wrapped in his scarf. He did it because Helen had found the $412,600 he took from the lodge over fifteen years and gave him until Monday to own up.**

How every significant clue fits:

- **E1 (The Wrong Bottle).** Helen went down for the 1991 and told everyone nobody else was to touch it. The bottle beside her is Aroha's cooking wine, and the magnum's slot is empty. Someone staged a fall with the nearest cheap bottle and took the real one. An accident does not remove a bottle from the room.
- **Aroha's Act I account.** Nobody passed her door after Helen, and there was exactly one smash, at about 21:49, nine minutes after Helen went down. That smash was the staging, not the fall. E3 backs her position: she is never out of shot for more than 20 seconds. Her view stops at the corner, so the cellar door could still be reached from the front hall; in Act I, Lionel, Dougal and Tessa all left by the hall door.
- **E2 (The Storm Hatch).** This settles the route: the killer came and went by the hatch. The bolt was drawn from inside beforehand. The wet black-sand prints of a nicked leather heel go from the hatch to the foot of the stairs and back.
- **E3 (The Time-lapse).** Three people left the table: Lionel 21:42–21:57, Dougal 21:45–21:56, Tessa 21:50–21:59. Tessa was seen going up and coming down the main stairs, so her toilet story is false. Lionel left with a red tartan scarf and came back wet, without it, in lodge slippers.
- **E7 (The Sandy Brogues).** Lionel's own shoes (he is the only one in slippers, he says he left his shoes in the boot room, and Tessa recognises them) are full of black ironsand. The car park is gravel, so his "car" story cannot explain the sand. The nicked left heel matches the prints in E2. The soaked long oilskin matches the figure Dougal saw.
- **E8 (The Magnum in the Scarf).** The weapon, with a smear and grey hairs on the base, hidden beside the hatch and wrapped in the scarf Lionel wore out of the dining room.
- **E6 (Helen's Note) with TT2, TD2 and TL5.** The money went through the books, which only Lionel kept. "Forty years" fits Lionel, not Dougal (twelve years). Tessa heard "Monday. Not a day later." at 18:20 while Dougal was in the lounge, and then saw Lionel leave the office. Lionel admits the "loans" when shown the note.
- **TA1 (Aroha).** Aroha bolted the hatch on Thursday. Lionel went down to the cellar at 18:40 and was out of sight for four or five minutes. That is when he drew the bolt, as he admits at TL3.
- **TT1 (Tessa) and TD1 (Dougal).** Two independent sightings of the same torch: stopped at the woodshed at about 21:51, then on the north path heading for the front at about 21:53, then the front door at about 21:54. This is Lionel's route back.
- **TL3 / TL4 (Lionel's last account).** He admits the bolt, the hatch, the argument, the money, the magnum, the scarf and the woodshed. He claims she slipped. That fails on three counts. He will not explain the smashed cooking bottle, which only he could have broken, and an accident needs no staging. She fell forward onto the steps, but her injury is at the back of her head, and her hair is on the base of the magnum. And he hid that magnum in a woodpile instead of calling for help.
- **E4 and TA2.** Aroha's imported whitebait: true, embarrassing, irrelevant.
- **E5 and TD1.** Dougal's call to the buyer: true, shameful, and his alibi.

**Reveal narration (read after the accusation; it contains nothing that is not already on a card or in testimony):** "At twenty to seven, Lionel Fitch went down to admire a bottle of wine and slid back a bolt. At twenty to ten he said he was going to his car. He went to the sea side instead, came in through the storm hatch and asked his oldest friend for more time. She said no and turned her back on him. He picked up the bottle she had saved for thirty-one years. Then he smashed the cooking wine beside her, wrapped the magnum in his scarf, and walked back round the house in the rain. He left the scarf in the woodpile and the sand in his shoes. The suspects remember. The evidence stays true."

## 12. Alternative theory

**The theory:** Dougal Price killed Helen. He faces ruin, the price cut cost him the money he needed, and he shouted at Helen at 21:20. Her note about "money" and "Monday" was meant for him. He slipped away at 21:45 and lied about where he went.

**Clues that point to it:**
- The 21:20 argument, which every suspect reports.
- His own Act I line: "My company's had a bad year, and every dollar matters." Tessa's opinion, if asked, that he is "in a hole" with money.
- E2: the hatch prints are a man's leather-soled shoe, about size 11. Dougal is a man in leather dress shoes, and if asked he says they are size 11.
- E3: Dougal away from 21:45 to 21:56.
- His Act I lie that he sat in the lounge.
- E6 read on its own: money, a deadline, an unnamed "you".

**Evidence that disproves it:**
- **E5 + E3:** the corded office phone was in use from 21:46 to 21:55, calling the buyer. Tessa had not yet left the table. Only Dougal (21:45–21:56) and Lionel (21:42–21:57) were away for the whole call, and Lionel came back wet with ironsand on his shoes (E3, E7), so he was outside, not at the desk. TD1 confirms the caller was Dougal.
- **Aroha's Act I account:** the single smash at about 21:49 happened while Dougal was on that call.
- **E2 + E7:** the nicked heel in the hatch prints matches Lionel's sandy brogues, not Dougal's dry shoes.
- **E6 + TD2 + TT2:** "Forty years" does not fit Dougal (twelve years). He has never seen the books. When Helen said "Monday" at 18:20, Dougal was in the lounge.
- **TD1:** Helen's death freezes the sale, so it ruins Dougal rather than saving him.

Note: the label on the cooking bottle in E1, her kitchen next to the cellar door and her lost job also cast passing suspicion on Aroha. This is not a second theory; E3 clears her at once (she is never out of shot for more than 20 seconds from 21:41 to 21:59).
